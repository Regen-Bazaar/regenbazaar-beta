Beach cleanups, mangrove planting, animal rescue: Southeast Asia

0:05 to 1:00. Along the coasts of Southeast Asia, small groups do real environmental work every week. They clean beaches. They plant mangroves. They rescue animals. The work is real. But when it is done, they have nothing they can sell. Carbon registries are built for large projects. Certification costs more than a small group earns. Grants want a proposal before the work and a long report after it. Donations depend on goodwill, and the donor gets a receipt, not proof. So the people closest to the problem earn the least from solving it. When the volunteers run out of time and money, the work stops. Our question was simple: what if finished impact could be sold, like any other product?
